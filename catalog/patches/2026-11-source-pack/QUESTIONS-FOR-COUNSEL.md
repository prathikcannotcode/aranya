## Q1. Schedule II: head (g) or head (k) for a restoration-scaled figure

Aranya's methodology, paper brief, sample finding and argument skill place a REA/HEA restoration figure under head (g). The catalog summary of Schedule II stopped at head (h), so head (k) never came up. No position is taken here.

**The text** (`ngt-schedule-2`, Gazette p.16):

> (g) Expenses incurred by the Government for any administrative or legal action or to cope with any harm or damage, including compensation for environmental degradation and restoration of the quality of environment;

> (k) Claims including cost of restoration on account of any harm or damage to environment including pollution of soil, air, water, land and eco-systems;

**Provisions that frame the choice:**

- s.15(1)(c): the Tribunal may by order provide "for restitution of the environment for such area or areas, as the Tribunal may think fit".
- s.15(4): the Tribunal may "divide the compensation or relief payable under separate heads specified in Schedule II so as to provide compensation or relief to the claimants and for restitution of the damaged property or environment, as it may think fit".
- s.17(1): the person responsible is liable "under all or any of the heads specified in Schedule II, as may be determined by the Tribunal".
- s.18(2)(e): an application may be made by "any person aggrieved, including any representative body or organisation".
- s.24(1): an amount ordered "on the ground of any damage to environment" is remitted to the authority under s.7A(3) of the Public Liability Insurance Act 1991 "for being credited to the Environmental Relief Fund".

**Reading A: head (g) carries it.** The head names "compensation for environmental degradation and restoration of the quality of environment" in terms, and "including" can be read to widen the head beyond Government outlay. A REA/HEA figure prices the restoration of environmental quality, and "compensation for environmental degradation" can be read to reach interim loss of services, which is what the HEA debit measures.

**Reading B: head (k) carries it, and (g) belongs to Government.** Head (g) opens with "Expenses incurred by the Government". On this reading the "including" words describe kinds of Government expense, so the head suits a Government claimant and sums already spent, not a projected restoration cost claimed by a private applicant. Head (k) is tied to no claimant and names "cost of restoration on account of any harm or damage to environment", eco-systems included.

**What we need decided:**

1. Which head, or both, should Aranya plead by default for a restoration-scaled figure, and should the catalog and the `ngt-compensation-argument` skill say so?
2. Is head (g) open to a private or NGO applicant?
3. Does "cost of restoration" in head (k) cover compensatory restoration for interim loss (the HEA debit), or only primary restoration? If only primary, where does interim loss sit: head (g), s.15(1)(c) directly, or nowhere?
4. Can (g) and (k) be pleaded together without double recovery?
5. Does s.24(1) send a restoration award to the Environmental Relief Fund, and how does that sit with a site-specific restitution order under s.15(1)(c), with the CPCB rule that damage-track compensation is used only at the site (`cpcb-ec-2019`, clause 1.4.2), and with the NGT practice of deposit with a Pollution Control Board (`rhythm-county-2026`)?

## Q2. Limitation: s.15(3) against s.14(3)

- s.14(3): no application "under this section" unless made "within a period of six months from the date on which the cause of action for such dispute first arose", plus up to sixty days for sufficient cause.
- s.15(3): no application for compensation, relief or restitution "under this section" unless made "within a period of five years from the date on which the cause for such compensation or relief first arose", plus up to sixty days for sufficient cause (`ngt-s15-3`).

A run working from the old catalog could apply six months to a restoration claim and call it dead. In the Morvali Creek fixture the harm began in 2024 and a fish kill followed in 2026.

1. Where one application seeks both settlement of a dispute under s.14 and restitution under s.15, which period governs which relief?
2. When harm continues or recurs, does each discharge or kill start a fresh cause, or does time run from when harm first arose?
3. Does the sixty-day proviso exclude the Limitation Act 1963, including its rule on continuing wrongs?
4. Search results say *Mantri Techzone v. Forward Foundation* (2019) treats s.14(3) and s.15(3) as separate periods, and that the Supreme Court issued notice on a challenge to the Act's limitation provisions. Neither was opened. Please confirm both and their current status.

## Q3. Appeal against an EC under s.16

s.16 allows an appeal "within a period of thirty days from the date on which the order or decision or direction or determination is communicated to him", plus up to sixty days (`ngt-s16`). Clause (h) covers "an order made ... granting environmental clearance in the area in which any industries, operations or processes or class of industries, operations and processes shall not be carried out or shall be carried out subject to certain safeguards under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986".

1. For a third party, when is an EC "communicated": newspaper publication, upload to a Government portal, or display at the panchayat under EIA para 10(i) (`eia-2006-para-10`)?
2. Does clause (h) reach every EC under the 2006 Notification, or only clearances in areas restricted by an EPA notification?
3. Once the thirty plus sixty days have run, can the validity of the EC be raised in an application under s.14 or s.15? Does the second proviso to s.18(2) matter where the applicant also appealed?

## Q4. Wrong appraising authority and false Form 1 (General Condition, para 8(vi))

The General Condition sends a Category B project within 5 km of a protected area to Central appraisal (`eia-2006-gc`, as substituted in 2014). Para 8(vi) makes deliberate concealment or false information material to appraisal a ground to reject the application or cancel the EC. The regulatory authority decides that after a personal hearing (`eia-2006-para-8-vi`).

1. If a SEIAA appraised a project the GC sent to the Centre, is the EC void, voidable, or curable by remand?
2. Where Form 1 answers "None" and a sanctuary lies within 5 km, is that "deliberate concealment" material to appraisal? Can the NGT act on para 8(vi) in an appeal, or must the regulator decide first?
3. Items 8(a) and 8(b): S.O. 523(E) of 29 January 2025 says the General Conditions do not apply, and the Supreme Court upheld it except Note 1 to entry 8(a) (`credai-gc-2025`). Please confirm the notified text and the 5 August 2025 order, and whether projects cleared before 2025 are affected.

## Q5. Defects in the public hearing (Appendix IV)

Appendix IV requires a minimum of 30 days' notice, advertisement in one major national daily and one regional vernacular daily or official State language, and a Summary EIA in English and the local language (`eia-2006-appendix-iv`). The Morvali fixture has 12 days' notice, one English paper and no local-language summary.

1. Does a defect of this kind vitiate the hearing and the EC, or must prejudice be shown?
2. What is the usual remedy: a fresh hearing, remand for appraisal, or quashing the EC?
3. Which authorities govern? None was opened for this pack.

## Q6. Post facto EC after *Vanashakti* (2026)

The May 2025 judgment was recalled in November 2025 (`credai-vanashakti-2025`). The rehearing on 29 July 2026 (`vanashakti-2026`) held the prior EC regime mandatory, upheld the closed 2017 Notification, quashed the 2021 OM with prospective effect, protected ECs already granted, let pending applications run to their conclusion, and barred future post facto EC except by a valid notification under EPA s.3. It left the Court's Article 142 power intact.

1. For a project that started without EC and has no pending 2017 or 2021 application, what route remains other than closure, demolition or a future notification?
2. Is a review or clarification of the 2026 judgment pending? The search found none.
3. MoEFCC reportedly directed resumed processing of pending applications in October 2026. Please confirm its terms.

## Q7. Positioning REA/HEA against the CPCB method and the Supreme Court cases

The proposal in `README.md` treats REA/HEA as a disclosed method for the CPCB "damage track". That track covers instances (d) to (f), where compensation is decided on detailed investigation by expert institutions and includes "compensation towards loss of ecology". It does not treat REA/HEA as a rival to the PI x N x R x S x LF formula, which applies to consent and direction violations.

1. Is a REA/HEA restoration figure additional to a formula-based or project-cost-based award, or an alternative to it? In *Rhythm County* (para 42) the NGT kept a remediation bank guarantee separate from compensation for distinct violations.
2. Do the eight principles in *Amravati* (para 17), stated for guidelines under the Solid Waste Management Rules 2026, bind the NGT when it quantifies compensation under ss.15 and 17?
3. Does the *Lodhi Property* requirement of subordinate legislation reach the NGT, or only Pollution Control Boards under Water Act s.33A and Air Act s.31A? The 21 May 2026 clarifying order is known only as quoted in an NGT order, and a press report says the opposite (`dpcc-lodhi-2026-order`).
4. CPCB clause 1.3.2 says CPCB "shall list the expert institutions" for damage cases. Must an applicant's REA/HEA expert come from such a list, or can the NGT weigh any qualified expert? s.19(3) frees the Tribunal from the Evidence Act.
5. Is anything known of an NGT or Indian court order that used HEA or REA? The search found none, which is not proof of absence.
