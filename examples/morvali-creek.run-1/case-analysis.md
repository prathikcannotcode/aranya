## Forum and jurisdiction

**NGT: yes, for the discharge.** The harm is effluent reaching the creek through a bypass at Survey No. 214. That arises under the Water Act 1974 and the EPA 1986, the parent of the EIA Notification, and both are in Schedule I (F-0001).

**Wild Life Act: not in this forum.** Schedule I lists seven enactments and the 1972 Act is not one. Lead with it and the Company's first objection is jurisdiction, on page one (F-0002). Any prosecution belongs in a criminal complaint, and standalone wildlife relief in a High Court writ. The file also gives you less than the note assumes. Nothing shows that birds have left: the surveys score benthic diversity, fish density and water quality (FL-0007). Nothing puts the 42 ha polygon inside the sanctuary either.

Nilapani still does work here, as a fact. It shows the Form 1 answer was false, it supports the precautionary case, and it may decide who should have granted the EC. If a bird survey comes in, harm to birds can follow as a consequence of the Water Act pollution, since the catalog's Schedule II summary includes harm to fauna.

The Sangh's letter of 9 October 2024 is the difficulty. It records black water since the last week of September and dead fish on 2 and 6 October. We need that letter to show SPCB's inaction, so it goes in, and the Company will read it.

*Bhavani Sugars* cannot carry this. A rule that time restarts with each discharge would read "first" out of the section, so it needs authority someone has actually read. I can't say whether the case exists. The instructing note's own header marks every citation in the file as invented, which makes the check compulsory. I treat the proposition as unsupported (FL-0004).

What I would plead instead: the fish kill of 14 July 2026 as a distinct cause of action, with the earlier history set out openly, and limitation not asserted as clear (F-0003). If s.15(3) reads as I recall, compensation and restitution are safe either way.

Closure is the exposed relief. It depends on s.14 and does not appear in the catalog's s.15 summary. If the Tribunal holds the s.14 period has run, the fallback is a High Court writ directing SPCB to enforce its own unmet direction.

## The EC

Take it out of this application. G4 points to a s.16 appeal, and my understanding, unconfirmed because s.16 is not in the catalog, is that an appeal cannot be folded into a s.14 application. The dates are also bad: SEIAA uploaded on 16 February 2024, the Company's notices ran on 19 February, and the Sangh's secretary had attended the hearing in September 2023.

The defects themselves are serious (see `eia-review.md`). Form 1 denies any protected area within 15 km while the EIA report puts a sanctuary at 3.2 km. My recollection, unconfirmed, is that the General Condition in the EIA Schedule makes a project within 10 km of a sanctuary Category A, which would mean SEIAA was the wrong authority (FL-0010).

The routes that remain, for your decision:

1. **Revocation request to SEIAA, copied to MoEFCC.** The Form 1 undertaking accepts revocation if any part is found false. I recommend sending it now. Whether a refusal starts a fresh appeal period is an open question for you.
2. **Writ petition in the High Court.** Grounds: wrong authority and false Form 1. Expect delay and alternative-remedy objections, given knowledge since February 2024.

One tension to settle before either: S1 is our best ground and it lives in the EC. If the EC falls, S1 falls with it, though the mill would then have no clearance at all. The catalog's note also reports that *Vanashakti v. Union of India* (2025) restricted post-facto clearances. That is unconfirmed, and it matters if the Company seeks a fresh clearance after a quash.
