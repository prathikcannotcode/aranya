## 1. Jurisdiction

The claim arises under the Water Act 1974 and the EPA 1986, both in Schedule I, so s.14 is met for the discharge (F-0001). The Wild Life Act 1972 is not relied on, because it is not in Schedule I (F-0002). ## 2. The power

- **Section 15 clause (c)**: restitution of the environment "for such area or areas, as the Tribunal may think fit". The discretion over area is where a restoration scale fits.
- **Section 15 clause (a)**: relief and compensation to victims of pollution. This carries lost catch.
- **Section 17**: the person responsible for damage to the environment pays under the Schedule II heads, and the Tribunal may apportion where liability cannot be attributed to one person.
- **Schedule II head (g)**: "including compensation for environmental degradation and restoration of the quality of the environment". Use it in the alternative. The head is worded around expenses incurred by the Government, and our applicant is a private cooperative (FL-0008). The clause (c) order does not depend on it.

## 3. The principle and the facts

Section 20 says the Tribunal shall apply the polluter pays, precautionary and sustainable development principles. The catalog's authorities:

- *Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum v. Union of India*, (1996) 5 SCC 647: polluter pays and precaution are part of Indian environmental law.
- *Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India*, (1996) 3 SCC 212: the polluter pays for remediation of the affected area. It concerned absolute liability for a hazardous activity, and the file does not show this effluent is hazardous, so use it for the cost of remediation.
- *M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath*, (1997) 1 SCC 388: the polluter must bear the cost of reversing the ecological damage.

The record supplies the facts. The Company told the fishers at the hearing of 2 September 2023 that the mill would run on zero liquid discharge. The EIA relied on that (4.3) and S1 required it. SPCB then found a bypass from the equalisation tank to the creek on 22 October 2024 and directed its removal within 15 days. The outfall was running on 3 February 2026. About three tonnes of fish died on 14 July 2026, and SPCB found the bypass in use on 16 July. On 28 April 2026 the Company had told SEIAA that no effluent leaves the premises.
